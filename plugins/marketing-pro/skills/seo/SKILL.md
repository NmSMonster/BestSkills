# SEO

Modern SEO is not tricks — it's being genuinely the best, most accessible answer to what a searcher wants, and making that legible to search engines. Google rewards content that satisfies intent and demonstrates real expertise; it penalizes manipulation. So the strategy is: pick the right questions to answer, answer them better than the competition, and remove technical friction. Everything below serves that.

## Step 1 — Keyword & intent research (what to write about)

Ranking starts with targeting the right query — one with demand you can realistically win.
- **Find the queries**: seed terms → expand with tools (Search Console for what you already get, autocomplete, "people also ask", keyword tools for volume). Group into topics, not isolated words.
- **Judge winnability**: volume (is anyone searching?) × difficulty (can you outrank who's there?) × relevance (does it bring *your* audience?). A lower-volume term you can rank #1 for beats a high-volume one you'll never crack. Long-tail, specific queries convert better and are easier to win.
- **Nail the search intent** — this is the crux. For each target query, look at what actually ranks now; that reveals what Google thinks the searcher wants:
  - **Informational** ("how to X") → guide/tutorial.
  - **Commercial** ("best X", "X vs Y") → comparison/review.
  - **Transactional** ("buy X", "X pricing") → product/landing page.
  - **Navigational** ("BrandX login") → the specific page.
  - Matching intent is non-negotiable: a sales page will never rank for a "how to" query, no matter how optimized. Give the searcher the *type* of thing they want.

## Step 2 — On-page SEO (making the page the best answer)

- **Title tag**: the single most important on-page element. Include the primary keyword naturally, near the front, compelling enough to earn the click (~50–60 chars). It's both a ranking factor and your ad in the results.
- **Meta description**: doesn't directly rank, but drives click-through — write it like ad copy with the benefit and keyword.
- **One H1** stating the topic; **H2/H3** structuring the content around subtopics and the questions searchers ask (mine "people also ask"). Headings are for readers first, structure second.
- **Content that fully satisfies intent**: cover the topic comprehensively — answer the main question *and* the follow-ups a searcher has, so they don't bounce back to Google (that "return to search" is a negative signal). Depth and completeness beat length padding.
- **Keyword usage**: natural. Include the term and its variants/synonyms where they fit; write for humans. Keyword-stuffing is a penalty, not a boost — if it reads awkwardly, it's hurting you.
- **Answer the query early** (featured-snippet friendly): a concise direct answer near the top, then the depth. Structure for the snippet (a clear definition, a list, a table).
- **Internal links** to related content with descriptive anchor text (spreads authority, helps discovery); **external links** to authoritative sources (signals credibility).
- **Images**: descriptive alt text (accessibility + image search), compressed, meaningful filenames.

## Step 3 — E-E-A-T and content quality

Google increasingly rewards **Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust** — especially for topics affecting health, money, safety (YMYL). Demonstrate it: real author expertise/bylines, first-hand experience, cited sources, accurate up-to-date facts, and genuine usefulness. Thin, generic, or AI-spun content that adds nothing over what's already ranking will not win — the bar is *better than the current best result*, not *exists*.

## Step 4 — Technical SEO (removing friction)

Great content can't rank if search engines can't crawl, index, or render it well:
- **Crawlability/indexing**: sensible `robots.txt`, an XML sitemap, no accidental `noindex` on pages you want ranked, clean canonical tags (avoid duplicate-content splits).
- **Site speed / Core Web Vitals**: load speed, layout stability, interactivity — a ranking factor and a bounce factor. Optimize images, minimize blocking resources.
- **Mobile-first**: Google indexes the mobile version — it must be fully functional and fast on phones.
- **HTTPS**, clean readable URLs (`/guide/seo-basics` not `/p?id=8817`), no broken links.
- **Structured data** (schema.org): mark up articles, products, FAQs, reviews → richer results (stars, FAQ dropdowns) and better understanding.
- **Site architecture**: logical hierarchy, important pages few clicks from the home page, related content interlinked into topic clusters.

## Step 5 — Off-page & measurement

- **Backlinks** remain a major authority signal — earned through genuinely link-worthy content, not bought (buying links risks penalties). Quality (relevant, authoritative domains) over quantity.
- **Measure and iterate**: Search Console (impressions, clicks, position, which queries), analytics (organic traffic, engagement, conversions). SEO is a long game — track trends over months, double down on what ranks, improve what's stuck on page 2.

## Deliverable

For a content/page task: the target query and its intent, the title tag and meta description, an outline with H1/H2/H3 structured around the intent and related questions, the primary/secondary terms to use naturally, internal-link and schema recommendations, and any technical fixes spotted. For an audit: prioritized issues (intent mismatch and technical blockers first, since they cap everything else), each with the fix and expected impact.

## Rules

- Match search intent before optimizing anything else — it's the ceiling on ranking.
- Write for humans and satisfy the query completely; keyword-stuffing and thin content are penalties.
- The bar is "better than the current top result," not "covers the keyword."
- Fix technical blockers (crawl, index, speed, mobile) — perfect content that can't be crawled ranks nowhere.
- No black-hat shortcuts (bought links, cloaking, spun content) — they win briefly and lose hard when caught.
