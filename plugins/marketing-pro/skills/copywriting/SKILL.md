# Copywriting

Copy has one job: move a specific reader to a specific action. It's not literature and it's not a feature list — it's persuasion aimed at a person with a problem. The best copy is often the plainest: clear beats clever, and the reader's self-interest beats your product's specs every time.

## Step 0 — Know these three things before writing a word

1. **Who** is the reader — their situation, their problem, what they already believe, what they're skeptical of. Copy written for "everyone" persuades no one. Write to one specific person.
2. **What action** you want (buy, sign up, click, book, reply) — exactly one primary action per piece.
3. **Why they'd hesitate** — the objections and doubts. Great copy is mostly objection-handling in disguise.

## The core principle — benefits, not features

People don't buy features; they buy a better version of their life. Translate every feature into what it *does for the reader*:
- Feature: "256-bit encryption." Benefit: "Your data stays yours — even we can't read it."
- Feature: "Syncs across devices." Benefit: "Pick up exactly where you left off, anywhere."
- The test: after every feature, ask **"so what?"** until you reach something the reader actually cares about. That's the benefit. Lead with it.

Deeper still: sell the *outcome and the feeling* — not "project management software" but "leave the office knowing nothing slipped." Features are proof the benefit is real; they support, they don't lead.

## Structure that persuades

Use a proven flow rather than improvising. Two reliable ones:

**PAS** (great for problem-aware readers): **Problem** — name their pain so they feel understood → **Agitate** — make the cost of it vivid (what it's really costing them) → **Solution** — your product as the relief. 

**AIDA**: **Attention** (hook) → **Interest** (relevant benefit) → **Desire** (proof + outcome they want) → **Action** (the ask).

For a landing page: clear benefit-driven **headline** → subhead that expands the promise → the core benefits (as the reader's outcomes) → proof (social proof, specifics, guarantees) → objection handling → single strong **CTA** (repeated). Above the fold must answer "what is this, what's in it for me, what do I do next" in seconds.

## Headlines (where 80% of the value is)

Most readers decide from the headline alone. Make it earn the next line:
- Lead with the biggest benefit or the sharpest hook. Specific beats vague ("Cut your AWS bill 40% in a weekend" beats "Optimize your cloud costs").
- Techniques that work: a specific number/result, a bold promise you can back, a provocative question the reader answers "yes" to, or naming their exact pain.
- Write 10 headlines, pick the best. The first one is rarely the winner.
- Never clickbait a promise the page doesn't keep — the click is worthless if it bounces.

## Voice and craft

- **Clear and concrete over clever.** Cleverness that obscures the message fails; a pun the reader has to decode is a speed bump. Plain, vivid, specific wins.
- **"You" more than "we."** The reader cares about themselves, not your company. Count the you's vs. we's — flip the ratio.
- **Short.** Short sentences. Short paragraphs. Cut every word that isn't working. "In order to"→"to". Ruthless editing is where copy gets its power.
- **Active, present, concrete verbs.** "Ship faster" not "increased shipping velocity can be achieved."
- **Specifics build belief.** "Join 12,000 teams" beats "join many happy customers." Numbers, names, and details are credible; adjectives ("amazing", "best-in-class") are noise the reader discounts.
- Match the brand voice and the audience's register — B2B enterprise and DTC consumer speak differently.

## The CTA

- **One primary action**, stated as a specific verb-first instruction ("Start your free trial", not "Submit"). Reduce it to the smallest low-risk next step.
- Reinforce it: what happens next, why now (urgency if genuine), risk reversal ("free, no card, cancel anytime"). Remove the friction and the fear.

## Proof and trust (copy that isn't believed doesn't convert)

Every claim needs backing: testimonials with real names/results, specific numbers, guarantees, logos, case results. Address the top objection head-on rather than hoping the reader forgets it. Honesty converts better than hype long-term — overpromising wins the click and loses the customer (and the refund/chargeback).

## Deliverable

The copy, plus: 2–3 headline options (headlines are worth testing), the single CTA, and a note on who it's written for and which objection each section handles. For anything meant to convert, recommend A/B testing the headline and CTA — copy is validated by response, not by opinion.

## Rules

- Benefits lead, features support — run the "so what?" test on every line.
- One reader, one action, one core message per piece.
- Clear beats clever; specific beats vague; "you" beats "we"; short beats long.
- Every claim backed by proof; no promise the product doesn't keep.
- Match brand voice; write copy to be *responded to*, then test it.
