# Content Strategy

Content without strategy is just noise you pay to produce. A strategy connects **a business goal** to **an audience's needs** through **the right content on the right channels**, and measures whether it worked. The most common failure is producing content nobody asked for, nobody finds, and nobody can tie to an outcome — "we should post more" is not a strategy.

## Step 1 — Goal and audience first

- **Business goal**: what is content actually for? Awareness (reach new people), acquisition (leads/signups), activation/education (help users succeed), retention (keep them), or authority (trust and reputation). Different goals → different content, channels, and metrics. Pin this down or everything downstream is unanchored.
- **Audience**: who are you serving, what do they care about, what problems/questions do they have, where do they already spend attention, what stage of awareness are they at (don't know they have the problem → comparing solutions → ready to buy)? Content must meet them at their stage.
- **The intersection** is your content territory: the overlap between *what the audience wants to consume* and *what advances your goal and where you have credibility*. Content outside that overlap — interesting to you but off-goal, or on-goal but boring to them — is wasted effort.

## Step 2 — Content pillars and formats

- **Pillars**: 3–5 recurring themes you'll own — topics central to your audience's needs and your expertise. Pillars give focus (you become known *for something*) and make planning tractable. Everything you make ladders up to a pillar.
- **Map to the funnel**: top-of-funnel (broad, valuable, discoverable — attracts and builds trust), middle (deeper, comparison, education — nurtures), bottom (product-specific, proof, conversion). A healthy mix; most brands over-produce bottom-funnel sales content and starve the top that actually brings new people.
- **Format to fit channel and message**: long-form article/guide (depth, SEO, authority), video (demonstration, reach, engagement), short social (awareness, personality), email (owned audience, nurture), infographic/carousel (digestible), webinar/podcast (depth + relationship). Pick format by what the message needs and where the audience is — not by what's trendy.

## Step 3 — Channel strategy (owned / earned / borrowed)

- **Owned** (your site, blog, email list) — you control it and it compounds; SEO content and email are assets that keep paying. **Prioritize building owned audience** — rented audiences (social followers) can vanish with an algorithm change.
- **Borrowed** (social platforms) — reach and discovery, but you play by their rules and pay in constant feeding. Use to attract people *toward* your owned channels.
- **Earned** (press, mentions, shares, backlinks) — credibility you can't buy, earned by genuinely valuable or notable content.
- **Don't spread thin**: better to dominate one or two channels where your audience actually is than to be mediocre everywhere. Pick based on where the audience is and where you can sustainably produce.

## Step 4 — The editorial calendar and cadence

- **Sustainable cadence over heroic bursts.** Consistency compounds; a burst followed by silence doesn't. Pick a rhythm you can actually maintain (one great weekly piece beats five rushed ones then a month off).
- **Plan a calendar**: topics mapped to pillars and funnel stages, tied to timing (launches, seasons, events), with the channel and format for each. Leave room for reactive/timely content.
- **Batch and systematize** production so it survives busy weeks.

## Step 5 — Distribution (the half everyone skips)

**Creating content is half the job; distribution is the other half — and it's the one people neglect.** A great piece nobody sees fails. For every piece, plan how it gets discovered:
- **Repurpose across formats**: one pillar piece → social posts, an email, a video script, a carousel, quote graphics. Create once, distribute many times — this is the highest-leverage move in content.
- **Push through every channel**: email it, post it, syndicate it, share in relevant communities. Don't publish and pray.
- Design for discovery: SEO for search (see the `seo` skill), shareability for social, subject lines for email.
- Rule of thumb: spend as much effort distributing a piece as making it.

## Step 6 — Measure and iterate

- **Metrics that map to the goal**, not vanity: awareness → reach/impressions/new visitors; acquisition → leads/signups/conversion; retention → returning readers, engagement; authority → backlinks, branded search, shares by credible people. Views alone mean nothing if they don't serve the goal.
- **Find what works and do more of it.** Track which pillars/formats/channels drive the goal, and reallocate toward winners. Most content underperforms and a few pieces carry the results — double down on the winners, retire the dead weight.
- Content is a long game: judge trends over months, not single posts.

## Deliverable

A content strategy: the goal and target audience, 3–5 content pillars, the channel priorities (and why those), format mix across the funnel, a sustainable cadence with a sample editorial calendar, a distribution/repurposing plan per piece, and the metrics you'll track against the goal. Right-size it — a solo creator gets a lean one-pager; a company gets the full plan.

## Rules

- Start from goal + audience; content that serves neither is cut.
- Distribution is half the work — plan it for every piece, repurpose relentlessly.
- Consistency you can sustain beats bursts you can't.
- Prioritize owned audience; use borrowed channels to feed it.
- Measure against the goal, not vanity metrics; double down on the few winners.
